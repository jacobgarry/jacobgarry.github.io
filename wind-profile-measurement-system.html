Project Overview

Turning point measurements into a repeatable flow map.

The system was developed to characterize the airflow within a custom wind chamber while reducing the time and positioning uncertainty of manual probe placement. A motorized traverse carried the sensing hardware through the test section, while embedded controls coordinated positioning and data collection.

Pitot probe→Differential pressure sensor→ESP32→Python / CSV

Design Evolution

From one-dimensional traversal to a refined two-axis scanner.

Each iteration addressed a distinct limitation: first automating horizontal motion, then adding vertical coverage, and finally improving the scanner’s structural support.

PROTOTYPE 01

Horizontal Traverse

The first iteration established automated motion along the chamber rail and integrated the Pitot probe, pressure sensing, stepper drive, and embedded electronics on a compact carriage. It replaced repeated manual repositioning with controlled point-to-point travel.

PROTOTYPE 02

Lattice-Supported Vertical Scanner

The second iteration added vertical travel and a lattice-supported tower around the lead-screw mechanism. Combined with the chamber rail, the system could position the probe across both horizontal distance and height.

2-Axis MotionAdditive ManufacturingSystem Integration
PROTOTYPE 03

Refined Two-Axis Scanner

The third iteration replaced the lattice tower with a cleaner guide-rod and vertical-drive arrangement. The redesigned probe carriage, exposed motion path, and integrated electronics produced the final measurement platform used for chamber mapping.

Test Environment

Custom wind chamber and traverse installation.

The aluminum-extrusion chamber housed the fan array, removable access panels, and the internal rail used by the automated measurement carriage.

Experimental Campaign

Systematic chamber characterization.

The completed platform executed a repeatable 5 × 5 mapping campaign across five longitudinal stations and five probe heights. Low-range differential pressure data were captured through the embedded acquisition chain and organized for downstream analysis.

Positioning

Horizontal and vertical motion placed the probe at prescribed test locations.

Sensing

A Pitot probe and low-range differential pressure sensor measured the chamber flow.

Acquisition

ESP32-based electronics streamed measurements for Python and CSV post-processing.

Prototype 3 operating inside the chamber.

Recorded Results

Measured wind-chamber velocity field.

The recorded V2 dataset was reduced into cross-sections, vertical and spanwise profiles, and flow-uniformity maps. The measured field spans approximately 1.8–3.9 m/s, with the plots showing a faster lower region and a lower-speed zone near the upper chamber boundary.

What the data shows: velocity becomes more uniform through much of the lower test region, while the upper portion remains slower across the sampled stations. These results demonstrate that the scanner captured repeatable spatial structure rather than only isolated speed readings.
