Active Airbrake Flight Control System

A servo-actuated aerodynamic braking system that used onboard sensing and real-time apogee prediction to determine when added drag was necessary during ascent.

System Architecture

Mechanical, embedded, and aerodynamic design in one assembly.

Exploded CAD assembly showing the deployable drag surfaces, geared actuation system, avionics stack, sensors, and structural interfaces.

Key Engineering Choices

Designed to respond only when the trajectory required it.

Real-Time Apogee Prediction

Onboard flight-state estimates were used to predict apogee and determine whether additional aerodynamic drag was needed.

Closed-Loop Deployment Logic

The system could extend, retract, or remain stowed instead of relying on a fixed deployment time.

Integrated Flight Hardware

Servo actuation, embedded computing, sensing, and mechanical deployment hardware were packaged into one removable assembly.

Analysis and Flight Data

Aerodynamic performance informed by simulation and telemetry.

CFD Flow Field

CFD was used to visualize the disturbed flow around the deployed surfaces and evaluate the source of added drag.

Pressure-Sensor Effects

Flight data showed that deployment changed the local pressure environment, highlighting the importance of pressure-port and sensor placement near aerodynamic control surfaces.

Altitude Award Winner

The competition vehicle finished six feet above its declared apogee. On that flight, the controller determined that added drag was unnecessary and correctly kept the airbrakes stowed—an important example of an active system choosing not to actuate when the flight state did not require it.
